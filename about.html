As a lil teen, I used to binge-watch social impact ads from India and Pakistan. Something about how they challenged norms and activated public conversation. And before going to bed, I typically had a chain of questions to interrogate mom & dad, until they were frustrated. That's probably where this started.

I grew up in a space where global and Western perspectives were more visible than local ones. Some realities felt distant even when they were close. Being from a minority background made questions of representation, power, and voice even more immediate. I became aware early on that meaning is never neutral, that it is deliberately molded, and that it can be remade as well.

That awareness and curiosity got me to study Communication Science at the University of Amsterdam. There, I explored how media, campaigns, news, entertainment, digital platforms, and the like are produced, received, and interpreted within social contexts. I also trained in empirical research methods, including surveys, experiments, content analysis, and qualitative methods to study how (communication) environments influence perception and behaviour.
